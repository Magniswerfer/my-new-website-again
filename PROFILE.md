# Site profile

The brief for the new site, based on the interview of 11 Nov 2026. Use it to make design and build decisions.

## In one line

A calm, editorial, bilingual (EN/DA) digital home for a personal reinvention. It changes often, is personal but curated, and is written in a reflective voice.

## The person behind it

A reading of Magnus, written from his own description (Nov 2026) and confirmed by him. It is the source for design decisions: when a detail is in doubt, check it against this.

### Reading Magnus

**Rooted, not restless.** Grew up in Aarhus and still lives there. He is starting over, but not running away, so the reinvention happens on home ground. The site shouldn't put on cosmopolitan polish. It should feel like someone at home in his own place.

**Found himself by stepping out of a given frame.** He went from a Christian school to a secular high school, where he "really found himself". He knows what it's like to inherit a structure and then choose his own. The site should have a firm structure that frees things rather than confines them, the way a good grid gives a page room to breathe.

**A nerd without apology, among friends who weren't.** Always the computer person in a group of non-nerds. The technical depth is real, but it's never gatekept: he explains, translates and includes. Technical detail should be visible and inviting, never insider-only.

**Design-literate, across the whole arc.** He studied IT Product Development at Aarhus University, and its design history course ran from Arts & Crafts to Frog Design. That span isn't one style; it's an argument running through the history:
- *William Morris:* make things honestly, by hand, against alienating industry. Morris was also a socialist.
- *Ulm and Dieter Rams (Braun):* form follows function. Less, but better. Design as a social good.
- *Frog:* form follows emotion. Objects should have character.

His taste sits where those meet: rigour and feeling, function and warmth. Braun and Rams are a favourite, "but that's not to say that's what the website design should all be. I like a human touch too." He belongs to all three, not one.

**The hand that fixes.** He builds things, does electronics and repairs what's broken in his house. His projects follow the same pattern:
- PadSquire frees a controller from Windows-only vendor software.
- Stages remembers what macOS forgets.
- Daily xkcd fills the days between strips.

Each one repairs a small gap someone else left. That's a right-to-repair instinct, and it lines up with his politics (very left wing) and with Morris. In design terms: honest construction, visible seams, nothing sealed shut.

**Worlds, not just stories.** He loved Star Wars and The Lord of the Rings growing up and reads a lot of fantasy and sci-fi. Those are built worlds: maps, appendices, calendars, lore, the "used future" of patched-up machines. Tolkien dated everything; Magnus keeps a week log. The fantasy isn't decoration to bolt on. It's a love of things with depth, history and wear, where details reward attention.

**Games as things to study.** He plays video games and studies them. Game designers obsess over "game feel": how an action responds when you press it. So motion on the site should answer the visitor, the way a good game answers input. Nothing should animate just to look busy.

**A teacher-researcher.** A PhD in computer science, specifically AI in education, means his work is about how people learn. That brings scaffolding, explanation, references and patience, and the academic habit of notes in the margin. He also loves music.

### Mind and hand

- **Mind:** historical, systematic, values-driven, imaginative, analytical, playful.
- **Hand:** a maker, tinkerer and repairer who prefers fixing to replacing and opening things up to sealing them.

**The throughline:** *well-made things people can understand, use, open up and fix, made with care and a bit of soul.* That runs from Morris through Ulm to open source, and it's the same whether he's repairing a shelf or writing a controller tool.

### What that means for the page

1. **Honest construction.** Show how the site is made, the way a repairable object shows its screws. The margin column becomes the workshop annotations, and each page shows how it was built.
2. **Function with feeling.** Rams's discipline in the structure, Frog's character in the details. Each detail earns its place by doing something.
3. **Patina, not polish.** Things show they've been used. The paper grain fits, and so would marks that build up as you use the site, like a pencil tick beside posts you've read.
4. **Depth for those who look.** Like a Tolkien appendix: the surface is calm, and there's more if you go looking (the week log, sidenotes, an index).
5. **Feel on input, stillness at rest.** Interactions respond crisply to the person, and nothing moves for show.
6. **Open and teachable.** Plain language, no tracking, accessible, readable source. Those are his politics expressed as craft.
7. **Rooted.** Danish week numbers, Danish dates, and Aarhus as a quiet fact, not a brand.

**Confirmed to lead the design:** all four threads: rigour + feeling, repair & openness, worlds & patina, and game feel.

**A mistake not to repeat:** turning one influence (Braun) into the whole design. Braun is one voice among several. Read the whole person, not the most quotable reference.

## Content

**Core sections**
- **About / story**: who I am now, and the shift that got me here
- **Writing / notes**: essays and shorter notes, the main thing that changes on the site
- **Projects / things I make**: work, side projects, experiments
- **Weeks**: a week log, one sentence a week about what I'm doing (replaced the Now page, which only repeated other pages). Links to elsewhere live in the footer

**Extras**
- **Photos / visual diary**: a photo stream
- **Bookshelf / media log**: books, music and films, with short notes
- **Newsletter + RSS**: ways to follow along
- **Contact form**: in addition to an email link

**Cadence:** weekly or more often. Adding things has to be fast and easy, or it won't happen.

**Openness:** personal but curated. Warm and human, with clear boundaries.

## Voice

- **Reflective**: quiet, essay-like, written in the first person
- Visitors should come away thinking it feels **thoughtful, curious, honest, crafted**
- Honest means I can say "I'm figuring this out"; crafted means the details are still deliberate

## Aesthetics

| | Direction | What it means in practice |
|---|---|---|
| **Mood** | Calm & editorial | Generous whitespace, a strong reading column, magazine-like rhythm |
| **Colour** | Off-white + ink | Warm paper background, near-black text, **one** accent colour used sparingly. Dark mode is the same idea inverted (ink background, paper text) |
| **Type** | Characterful display | One distinctive headline face that *is* the identity, paired with a quiet, very readable text face |
| **Motion** | A few signature moments | Mostly still. One or two memorable interactions (e.g. the homepage name/intro, the language switch, or page transitions). Everything else stays calm |

**Visual decisions (from three rounds of type/colour review):**
- **Colour:** *Strawberry Thief* (Morris's indigo and madder; replaced pine in Nov 2026). Light: linen `#ECEBE6`, indigo ink `#1C2233`, indigo `#2C4A8C`, madder `#A9362B`, muted `#5D6170`. Dark: `#12141C`, `#E7E6EF`, `#8FA8E8`, `#E06A5C`, `#9A9CAA`. Indigo is the working colour; madder only marks this week's light and marks of use
- **Text:** IBM Plex Sans for body copy, IBM Plex Mono for dates, navigation and labels
- **Headline:** **Schibsted Grotesk** (bold weights) for the name, headlines and section titles, with letter spacing `+0.05rem`

**Tension to keep in balance:** the calm editorial layout and the characterful display face pull in different directions. Use the display face loudly in a few places (name, section titles) and keep everything else quiet.

## Layout (after the "less AI" pass)

- **Weekly notebook:** every page has a left margin column for context (week numbers, dates, section names, the reading line) and a ~42rem reading column. Left aligned; the right side of wide screens stays open
- **Hero:** the latest week-log sentence as the big headline. The margin says "Week 46, 2026": the ISO week of that entry, since Danes date things by week number
- **Weeks grid (flair):** the year as 52 small squares under the hero label (and on the Writing page). Drawn as a speaker grille: weeks with something new are lit indigo and link to it; this week is a madder indicator light that glows; pointing at a week shows it in the readout line below; a nod to *Four Thousand Weeks*
- **Print texture (flair):** a fine fixed paper grain over the page, and every indigo mark printed twice, the second pass a pixel off, like a risograph
- **Indigo** only on underlines, rules, lit weeks and the reading line; **madder** only on this week and read marks. **Plex Mono** only for numbers in the margin
- **Avoided on purpose:** all-caps labels, an accent-coloured word in headlines, `→` on links, dot-joined meta strings, rounded cards

## Signature moments (launch)

1. **Underline → heading**: when a post link is clicked, its indigo underline lifts off and stretches into the rule under the post title. Going back reverses it. Built with the View Transitions API (Astro `<ClientRouter />`), with a plain page load as fallback
2. **Reading line**: on essays and long notes, a thin indigo line with a dot at its tip draws down the margin as you read

Both respect `prefers-reduced-motion` (no animation; the end state is shown). Considered and dropped: name settling in, living dot, bookshelf spines.

## Details from the reading (Nov 2026)

- **Indicator light:** the dot after the name is a steady indigo light
- **EN/DA slide switch:** the language link is a two-position switch; the knob slides, then the page changes
- **Keys:** buttons press down a pixel when used
- **Read marks:** a hand-drawn madder tick in the margin beside posts and weeks this visitor has already read (stored only in their browser)
- **Sidenotes:** in Keystatic, select words in a post and add a Sidenote; it sits in the margin beside the line, and opens in place on phones
- **Open the case:** a footer button (or the G key) shows how the page is built: grid outlines, baseline, heading levels and a card with the grid measured live and the palette

## The workshop book (Nov 2026 reimagining)

The site is a book from a small private press in the spirit of Morris, documenting a maker-scholar's work, with Braun-precise instruments set into its pages.

- **Open book (homepage):** a two-page spread. The left page (verso) is the *mind*: this week, the weeks grille, blog, rotation. The right page (recto) is the *hand*: projects and photos. A binding with a sewn thread runs between them; below two-page width the book closes to one page and the binding becomes a stitched seam
- **Instrument plates (projects):** each project is a nameplate held by four screws at different angles; pointing at a plate turns them a quarter, as if about to open it up
- **Endpaper (footer):** every page ends on deep indigo with a hand-drawn vine and madder berries; the one loud colour moment
- **Running head:** name, contents and a double rule at the top of every page
- **Press mark:** a leaf sprig (indigo, one madder berry) closes every post and project
- **Initials:** essays open with a three-line indigo initial

## Type hierarchy

| Level | Role | Style |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | One per page: homepage sentence, page title, post title | Schibsted Grotesk 800, large |
| 2 | List items: post, project and book titles; prose subheads | Schibsted Grotesk 600, 1.375rem |
| 3 | Reading text | IBM Plex Sans 400, 17px |
| Margin | Context only: section names, week labels, dates | IBM Plex Sans 500, 15px; numbers in Plex Mono |

The wordmark stays Schibsted 800 (it's the logo). Book titles in running text are italic. Pull quotes are italic with an indigo rule, never larger than a subhead.

## Technical direction

| | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| **Domain** | **magnushkaspersen.com** (primary). **magnus.hk** as a short alias that redirects to it, if the renewal at Alibaba goes through |
| **Framework** | Astro: static, fast, has built-in i18n routing and content collections |
| **Editing** | A simple CMS. Recommended: **Keystatic** or **Decap CMS**. Both are git-based, give a web UI for writing, and need no database |
| **Hosting** | **Cloudflare** Pages/Workers |
| **Contact form** | Cloudflare Worker / Pages Function, sent to email, with Turnstile for spam |
| **Newsletter** | An external service (e.g. Buttondown), plus RSS feeds from Astro |
| **i18n** | `/en/` and `/da/` routes. A post can exist in one language or both. Show a note when a translation is missing |
| **Photos** | Astro image optimisation; photos stored in the repo or in Cloudflare R2 if the library grows |
